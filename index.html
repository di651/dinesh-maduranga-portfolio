PROJECT 04

Counter-Flow Double-Pipe Heat Exchanger – CFD Analysis

Thermal-fluid simulation · ANSYS Fluent · Design verification

UNIVERSITY CFD PROJECT

Design objective< 45°CMeet cooling target with less material

Project overview

Completed as the 7850ENG Fluids and CFD final research simulation project at Griffith University. I investigated how the length of a counter-flow, concentric double-pipe heat exchanger affects cooling performance. The design objective was to cool the hot-water stream from 55°C to below 45°C while selecting the shortest practical exchanger length to limit copper-tube material use.

I first estimated the required length using the NTU–effectiveness method, which indicated approximately 0.46 m. I then used ANSYS Fluent to compare three lengths – 0.38 m, 0.46 m and 0.52 m – under the same boundary conditions and solver approach.

Model and operating conditions

  • Counter-flow concentric double-pipe arrangement with copper tubes.
  • Hot-water inlet: 55°C at 0.6 L/min.
  • Cold-water inlet: 25.4°C at 2.0 L/min.
  • Hot pipe: 4.73 mm inner diameter and 6.35 mm outer diameter.
  • Cold pipe: 11.08 mm inner diameter and 12.70 mm outer diameter.

Simulation workflow

  • Created the 3D concentric-tube geometry in ANSYS for a counter-flow arrangement.
  • Prepared a hexahedral-dominant mesh using face meshing and the sweep method along the pipe length.
  • Applied 30 edge divisions, face refinement and three inflation layers in the hot-fluid wall region.
  • Ran steady-state, pressure-based simulations in ANSYS Fluent using the energy equation and standard k–ε turbulence model.
  • Reviewed temperature and velocity contours, outlet temperatures and residual convergence.

Mesh and convergence check

The report compared a coarse mesh of 97,470 elements with a medium mesh of 888,784 elements for the 0.46 m case. The reported hot outlet temperature changed from 45.98°C to 44.28°C (3.84% difference). The medium mesh was selected for the final cases as a balance between solution detail and computational cost.

Engineering outcome: The 0.46 m case achieved the required outlet temperature of below 45°C and agreed with the analytical NTU–effectiveness length estimate. The 0.38 m case did not meet the target; the 0.52 m case provided additional cooling but was not the minimum-material option.

My analysis and interpretation

Compared thermal performance across the three lengths and reviewed temperature contours, velocity behaviour and solver residuals. The report recorded acceptable convergence for the selected medium mesh: energy residual below 10⁻¹⁰, momentum residuals below 10⁻⁸ and continuity stabilising near 10⁻⁵.

Assumptions and limitations

The simulation used steady-state conditions, constant material properties and a standard k–ε turbulence model. The cold-side Reynolds number was approximately 2,515, indicating transitional behaviour; the selected turbulence model does not represent transition exactly. These assumptions should be considered when interpreting the numerical results.
